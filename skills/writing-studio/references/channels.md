# Channels

One idea, four formats. The long-form article is the **source of truth**: it holds the depth, data and links. The other channels are overviews that send people to it.

## Blog article (search)

- **Goal:** rank for a keyword and fully answer the reader's question.
- **Length:** whatever the reader need requires. Explainers and research usually run 1,200 to 2,500 words, guides as long as the steps need.
- **Must have:** the full SEO checklist (`seo.md`), the reader-need structure, images with alt text, internal and external links, and a CTA.
- **Deliver:** the Markdown article, SEO title, meta description, slug, link table and image list.

## Substack post

Substack posts are read in the inbox first and found through search second.
- **Title** under 60 characters, plus a **subtitle** that adds the reason to read.
- **The first two lines** carry the hook, because they appear in the email preview and in Notes. Open with the result, tension or story, not with background.
- A **more personal voice** than the blog: first person is welcome, and so are opinions and behind-the-scenes detail.
- Short sections with clear H2s. Images break up the email.
- Set the **SEO title and description** in the post settings (same limits as the blog).
- **CTAs:** a subscribe button near the top for new visitors, and one main CTA at the end (reply, share, read the deep dive or watch the video).
- **Republishing a blog article:** add a short personal intro on why you wrote it, and use a canonical link to the original if your setup allows it.

## Newsletter issue

The newsletter is an **overview** that points to the deep dives.
- **Subject line** under 50 characters, plus **preview text** that completes it.
- **Structure:**
  1. **Hook** (2 to 3 sentences): the single most surprising result or idea this issue.
  2. **One section per piece** being promoted: 2 to 4 sentences, one key number or image, and a descriptive link ("Read the full experiment →").
  3. **One personal line:** what you're working on next, or a question for readers to reply to.
- **Length:** 300 to 600 words. Readers should finish it in about 2 minutes.
- **One primary CTA** per issue. Every link uses descriptive anchor text.
- Write it so it still reads well in plain text (some readers block images).

## YouTube video

The video is an overview with **demos**. The article holds the depth.

**Deliver:**
1. **Title** (see `titles.md`) and **3 thumbnail text options** of 2 to 4 words.
2. **Script outline** with timings:
   - **0:00 to 0:30, hook:** show the result or the surprising moment first, then say what the viewer will get. No "Hey guys, welcome back" preamble.
   - **Chapters:** one per main idea, each with one visual or live demo. Mention the article for the details.
   - **Re-hooks** every 60 to 90 seconds: a question, a result reveal or a "here's where it breaks".
   - **Outro (under 20 seconds):** one CTA (the article, the next video or subscribe) with a reason.
3. **Description:**
   - First 2 lines (about 150 characters): what the video shows, including the keyword. This is the part visible before "more".
   - Links: the full article, the code or repo, the newsletter.
   - **Chapters:** timestamps starting at `0:00`, at least 3 of them, each at least 10 seconds long, written as descriptive titles.
   - 3 to 5 relevant hashtags at most.
4. **Tags:** 5 to 10, from specific to broad.
5. **Pinned comment:** a question that invites replies, or the article link.

**YouTube checklist:**
- [ ] Keyword in the title and in the first line of the description.
- [ ] The hook shows the payoff within 30 seconds.
- [ ] Chapters added, starting at 0:00.
- [ ] The title, thumbnail and first 30 seconds make the same promise.
- [ ] The article and repo are linked in the description.
- [ ] The same voice rules as written content apply to the script: no antithesis cadence and no hype.

## Repurposing: one deep dive, every channel

1. **Write the deep-dive article first.** Run the full workflow and the SEO audit.
2. **Pull out the assets:** the 3 most surprising findings, the best chart, one quotable line and one demo.
3. **Newsletter:** a hook from finding 1, one paragraph per article, and links.
4. **YouTube:** each finding becomes a chapter, the demo becomes the visual, and the article goes in the description.
5. **Substack:** either the newsletter itself, or the article with a personal intro.
6. **Social (optional):** a short thread or post per finding, each linking to the article or video.

Every format links back to the deep dive, so search traffic, subscribers and viewers all flow to one place.
